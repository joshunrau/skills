# Exemplars

Use these synthetic passages to calibrate phrasing and density. Read the shared G examples and those matching the register of the draft. Rule IDs correspond to [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md) and its register references; example facts are illustrative.

---

## G4: Fronted reason for resistible conclusions

**Do:**

> Given that the supplier has missed three consecutive deadlines and the penalty clause takes effect next quarter, we recommend switching to the secondary vendor for the remaining deliverables.

**Don't:**

> We recommend switching to the secondary vendor for the remaining deliverables because the supplier has missed three consecutive deadlines and the penalty clause takes effect next quarter.

*The "don't" version buries the justification after the conclusion, which works for an uncontroversial explanation but weakens the persuasive force when the reader may resist the recommendation.*

## G5: Concession before committed claim

**Do:**

> While the pilot program exceeded its budget by 15%, the per-unit cost dropped steadily across all three phases, and the final phase came in under target.

**Don't:**

> The per-unit cost dropped steadily across all three phases, and the final phase came in under target. The pilot program exceeded its budget by 15%.

*The "don't" version states the positive finding first and then tacks on the negative as an afterthought. The "do" version concedes the budget overrun up front and closes on the committed claim.*

## G12: Inference hedged; verified fact stated flat

**Do:**

> The warehouse logged 340 shipments on Tuesday, up from 280 the previous week. The increase appears to be driven by the holiday pre-order window, though we have not confirmed this with the sales team.

**Don't:**

> The warehouse seems to have logged around 340 shipments on Tuesday. The increase is driven by the holiday pre-order window.

*The "don't" version hedges a verifiable count and states an inference as a fact. The hedge should track verification status: the shipment count is known; the cause is inferred.*

## G14: Exact figures where known; vague quantifiers where not

**Do:**

> Of the 12 branches audited, 9 met the new safety threshold (a pass rate of 75%). The remaining three cited staffing shortages, though the specific gaps varied and we do not yet have detailed breakdowns.

**Don't:**

> Most branches met the new safety threshold. The remaining ones cited various staffing shortages, and the specific pass rate was around 75% or so.

*The "don't" version rounds where exact figures exist and feigns precision where they do not.*

## G16: Verdict first, reservations second

**Do:**

> Overall, the proposal is well structured and addresses the core requirements. There are two areas I would revise: the timeline for Phase 2 is optimistic given the procurement lead times, and the risk section does not account for the regulatory review.

**Don't:**

> The timeline for Phase 2 is optimistic given the procurement lead times, and the risk section does not account for the regulatory review. That said, the proposal is well structured and addresses the core requirements.

*The "don't" version leads with reservations and buries the positive verdict. Lead with the overall judgment when there is something positive to say.*

## E9: Apology formula

**Do:**

> I'm very sorry about the hold-up. I underestimated how long the compliance review would take.

**Don't:**

> I wanted to sincerely apologize for the delay. There were a number of factors that contributed to the timeline slipping, including the compliance review taking longer than expected, some internal scheduling conflicts, and the need to coordinate across departments. I hope this has not caused too much inconvenience.

*The "don't" version over-explains. The apology is one clause with the cause attached directly, then move on.*

## T3: Technical explanation: term, gloss, mechanism, consequence

> The system uses a message queue (a temporary holding area where tasks wait in line to be processed). When a user submits a request, it is placed at the back of the queue. A separate worker process picks up each task in order and completes it. This means that during peak load, requests are not lost; they simply wait longer.
