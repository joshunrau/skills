# Writing for Humans

Apply this guidance to the requested draft or document. Preserve its facts, intent, audience, and requested format. Treat stylistic devices as a repertoire: use them where they serve the passage, without changing commitments or certainty to fit a formula.

## References

Before drafting, read the reference for the requested deliverable:

- [Formal Documents](references/formal-documents.md): proposals, pitches, position papers, and responses to critique.
- [Email](references/email.md): emails and short messages to other people.
- [Technical Explanation](references/technical-explanation.md): explanatory passages within either register; read alongside the relevant document or email reference.

Read the shared examples and those matching the draft in [Exemplars](references/exemplars.md) to calibrate phrasing and density. For other written deliverables, use the shared guidance below and the references that fit the task.

## Shared Guidance

### Parentheses and Glosses

**G1.** Gloss unfamiliar terms in parentheses: "i.e.," for a restatement, "e.g.," for an instance. Match the gloss to the reader's knowledge.

**G2.** Use parentheses for asides, glosses, qualifications, and statistical results. Do not use em-dashes.

**G3.** Keep tangential remarks inside the sentence in parentheses; make a central point a main clause.

### Reasoning and Argument

**G4.** When a conclusion or request may meet resistance, put its reason first ("Given that X, Y"). For uncontroversial explanations, a trailing "since," "as," or "because" is natural.

**G5.** When disagreeing with something that has merit, concede that merit briefly ("While X, Y") and close on the committed claim. A refusal with nothing to concede can be flat.

**G6.** When defending a choice through precedent, put the precedent first ("Consistent with prior benchmarks, ..."; "As we discussed, ...").

**G7.** After presenting alternatives, close with the action or conclusion that holds under either ("In any case, ...").

### Sentence Devices

**G8.** Use sentence-initial stance or frame adverbs, set off by a comma, where they orient the reader. Register references supply the inventories.

**G9.** Within a multi-item reply or comparison, introduce a topic shift with a fronted phrase ("Regarding X,"; "In terms of X,").

**G10.** Attach an assessment of a just-stated fact as a trailing ", which ..." clause. Match its voice to the register.

**G11.** "As well as Z" is a recurring alternative to "and Z" at the end of a list.

Use "This means that ..." for a consequence with a clear referent; add a noun when the referent changes ("This decision ..."). "As to" is an occasional alternative to "about" or "regarding."

### Evidence and Attribution

**G12.** Hedge interpretations with "appears" or "seems"; state verified facts flatly. The hedge tracks verification status.

**G13.** Attribute relayed information to its source and preserve the source's degree of certainty.

**G14.** Use exact figures where known and explicit pairs for comparisons ("14 ms vs. 38 ms"). Use approximate or qualitative quantities when precision is unavailable.

**G15.** Recount a decision process through the choices considered, their criteria, and their outcomes. End with what was selected.

**G16.** Open an evaluation with the overall judgment, followed by specific reservations. Include the positive assessment when warranted.

**G17.** Open a drafted reply to a factual or yes-or-no question with the answer, then explain.

### Formatting

**G18.** Use title case for headings and subject lines.

### Wording Preferences

State conclusions and recommendations plainly, supported by concrete facts. Finish when the point is complete; omit vague corporate framing and aspirational closings that add no information.

Do not use "well taken," "I'm writing regarding," or "bear(s) this out."
